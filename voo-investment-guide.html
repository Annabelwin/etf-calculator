What Is VOO? A Beginner’s Guide

Learn how Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO) works and why many long-term investors use it.

VOO is the ticker symbol for the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF. It is one of the most popular exchange-traded funds in the world and is designed to track the performance of the S&P 500 Index.

In simple terms, buying VOO means investing in a fund that holds shares of many of the largest companies in the United States.

What is VOO?

VOO is an ETF created by Vanguard. Its goal is to mirror the return of the S&P 500, an index made up of 500 leading U.S. companies. Because of this, VOO gives investors broad exposure to the U.S. stock market through a single investment.

How does VOO work?

When you invest in VOO, you are not buying just one stock. Instead, you are buying a fund that represents many large U.S. businesses across different sectors. This makes VOO a simple and diversified option for long-term investors.

What is the long-term return of VOO?

Since VOO tracks the S&P 500, its long-term return is generally similar to the long-term return of that index. Many investors use an estimated annual return of around 8%–10% when planning future investment growth, although actual returns vary.

VOO vs SPY

VOO and SPY both track the S&P 500 index, so their performance is usually very similar. Investors often compare them based on expense ratios, liquidity, and fund structure. For long-term investors, VOO is often seen as a simple and cost-effective choice.

Is VOO a good long-term investment?

Many investors consider VOO a strong long-term investment because it offers diversification, low costs, and exposure to large U.S. companies. It is widely used for retirement accounts and passive investing strategies.
