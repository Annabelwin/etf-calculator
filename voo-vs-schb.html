VOO vs SCHB

VOO and SCHB are both low-cost ETFs that track the U.S. stock market, but they take slightly different approaches. VOO focuses on the S&P 500, while SCHB tracks the entire U.S. broad market.

The Key Difference Most Investors Care About

The biggest difference between VOO and SCHB is diversification. VOO tracks the S&P 500 index, which includes about 500 of the largest companies in the United States. These companies make up a large portion of the total market value of the U.S. stock market. SCHB goes further. It tracks a broad U.S. market index that includes large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap companies. In other words, SCHB gives investors exposure to almost the entire U.S. stock market, while VOO focuses only on the largest companies.

Which ETF Do Long-Term Investors Prefer?

Many long-term investors choose VOO because the S&P 500 already represents the majority of the U.S. stock market's value. Large companies like Apple, Microsoft, Amazon and Nvidia dominate the index, so owning the S&P 500 already provides strong exposure to the economy. However, some investors prefer SCHB because it also includes smaller companies that may grow faster over long periods. In practice, the performance difference between VOO and SCHB is often small because large companies drive most of the market returns.

Simple Portfolio Example

Many investors keep their portfolio simple by choosing just one broad ETF. For example:

100% VOO — simple exposure to the S&P 500 or 100% SCHB — exposure to the entire U.S. market

Both approaches can work well for long-term investors who continue investing regularly and stay invested through market cycles.
