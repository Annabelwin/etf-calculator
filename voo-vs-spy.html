What Most Investors Really Want to Know

If both funds hold the same index, why does anyone even compare them?

The honest answer is simple: for a long-term investor, the main issue is usually cost. For a trader, the main issue is often liquidity.

That is why these two ETFs keep showing up in the same conversation. They offer the same market exposure, but they are often used by different types of investors.

Why Long-Term Investors Often Prefer VOO

VOO is commonly chosen by buy-and-hold investors because it offers low-cost S&P 500 exposure in a very simple package.

If your plan is to keep buying every month or every quarter for many years, then an ongoing fee difference may matter more than small structural details.

In real life, many long-term investors are not looking for the most tradeable ETF. They are looking for the one they can hold calmly for 10 to 20 years.

Why SPY Is Still Extremely Popular

SPY remains one of the most heavily traded ETFs in the world. That matters a lot to traders, institutions and investors who care about liquidity, tight spreads and quick execution.

So SPY is not “bad.” It simply solves a slightly different problem.

If you trade often, SPY may make more sense. If you mainly build wealth slowly over time, many investors lean toward VOO.

A Practical Investor View

If you are a beginner and want one straightforward answer, VOO is often the easier choice for long-term investing.

If you are very active in the market and care about trading volume more than fee savings, SPY deserves attention.

The important thing is not choosing the “perfect” ETF. The important thing is choosing one that fits how you actually invest.

VOO vs SPY FAQ

Do VOO and SPY track the same index?

Yes. Both ETFs track the S&P 500 Index.

Why do long-term investors compare VOO and SPY?

They compare them because both offer similar market exposure, but may differ in cost, trading style and long-term preference.

Is VOO cheaper than SPY?

Yes. VOO is commonly known for having a lower expense ratio, which is one reason many long-term investors prefer it.

Which is better for buy-and-hold investing?

Many buy-and-hold investors lean toward VOO because of its lower ongoing cost and long-term investing appeal.
