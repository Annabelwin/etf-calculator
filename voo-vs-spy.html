VOO vs SPY

Which S&P 500 ETF may be better for long-term investors?

VOO and SPY are two of the most popular ETFs for investors who want exposure to the S&P 500. Because both funds track the same index, many people assume they are basically identical. In practice, their long-term performance is often very similar, but there are still a few meaningful differences investors should understand.

In simple terms: both VOO and SPY give you exposure to large U.S. companies, but investors often compare them based on cost, structure, trading activity, and long-term suitability.

What do VOO and SPY have in common?

Both VOO and SPY track the S&P 500 Index. That means each ETF gives investors broad exposure to many of the largest publicly traded companies in the United States. For someone building long-term wealth, both can serve as a simple core holding.

VOO vs SPY: the main differences

1. Expense ratio

One of the most common reasons long-term investors choose VOO is its lower expense ratio. Even a small difference in annual cost can matter over many years, especially for large portfolios and consistent monthly investing.

2. Trading style

SPY is often associated with very high trading volume and is widely used by active traders. VOO is often favored by buy-and-hold investors who care more about long-term compounding and low costs than trading flexibility.

3. Long-term investing preference

For many long-term investors, the decision often comes down to simplicity and cost. Since both track the same index, a lower-cost option may feel more attractive for investors planning to hold for many years.

Is VOO better than SPY for long-term investing?

Many investors believe VOO is slightly better suited for long-term investing because of its lower expense ratio. If your goal is long-term accumulation rather than frequent trading, that cost difference may matter more than small structural differences.

Is SPY still a good ETF?

Yes. SPY remains one of the most widely used ETFs in the world. It provides the same broad S&P 500 exposure and is often preferred by traders because of its liquidity and trading activity. The key issue is not whether SPY is good, but whether it matches your investing style.

Which one should beginners choose?

Many beginners focused on long-term investing may prefer VOO because it is simple, widely trusted, and commonly viewed as cost-efficient. Investors who care about active trading or market liquidity may pay closer attention to SPY.
