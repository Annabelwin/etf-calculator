What Actually Matters for Long-Term Investors

VOO and SPY both track the same index — the S&P 500. That means their holdings are nearly identical, and their performance is usually very close.

The biggest difference is cost. VOO has a lower expense ratio than SPY, which may seem small, but compounds over long periods of time.

SPY, on the other hand, is one of the most actively traded ETFs in the world. That makes it more useful for traders who need liquidity and flexibility.

So while performance looks similar, the real difference is how you plan to use the ETF.

Which One Fits Your Investing Style?

If you are building a long-term portfolio and investing consistently, VOO is often the better choice because of its lower cost.

If you are trading frequently or using advanced strategies, SPY may be more suitable due to its liquidity and trading volume.

For most long-term investors, minimizing cost tends to matter more than trading flexibility.

In short: VOO is better for long-term investing, while SPY is better for trading.

VOO vs SPY FAQ

Is VOO better than SPY?

For long-term investors, VOO is often better due to its lower expense ratio. For traders, SPY may be more useful because of its high liquidity.

Do VOO and SPY perform the same?

They are very similar because both track the S&P 500, but VOO may slightly outperform over time due to lower fees.

Why is SPY more popular for trading?

SPY has extremely high trading volume and liquidity, making it ideal for active traders and institutions.

Can I just choose one?

Yes. Most long-term investors only need one S&P 500 ETF, and VOO is often the simplest choice.
