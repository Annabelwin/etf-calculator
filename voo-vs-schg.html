VOO vs SCHG

Broad market vs growth ETF comparison

VOO and SCHG are both popular ETFs, but they serve different purposes in a portfolio. VOO tracks the S&P 500 and provides broad exposure to large U.S. companies across sectors. SCHG focuses on large-cap growth stocks and is designed for investors who want stronger exposure to growth-oriented companies.

In simple terms: VOO is broader and more balanced, while SCHG is more concentrated in growth stocks and may be more volatile.

What is the difference between VOO and SCHG?

VOO gives investors exposure to a broad group of large U.S. companies through the S&P 500. SCHG tracks a large-cap growth index and focuses more heavily on companies with strong growth characteristics. That means SCHG often has greater exposure to technology and other fast-growing sectors.

VOO vs SCHG: main differences

Diversification

VOO is generally more diversified because it includes large U.S. companies across multiple sectors. SCHG is more concentrated in growth-oriented businesses.

Growth potential

SCHG may deliver stronger performance during periods when growth stocks lead the market. However, that higher upside often comes with greater volatility.

Volatility

Because SCHG is more concentrated and growth-heavy, many investors view it as more volatile than VOO.

Portfolio role

Many investors use VOO as a core holding and SCHG as a growth tilt. Investors who prefer a simpler and broader approach may choose VOO alone.

Is SCHG better than VOO?

SCHG is not necessarily better than VOO. It may be more attractive to investors looking for stronger growth exposure, while VOO may be more attractive to investors seeking broad diversification and a simpler long-term core ETF.

Can you hold both VOO and SCHG?

Yes. Some investors combine VOO and SCHG to balance broad market exposure with additional growth exposure. The trade-off is overlap, because many growth stocks in SCHG are already present in VOO.

Which one is better for beginners?

Many beginners may prefer VOO because it is broad, simple, and easy to understand. Investors who want more growth exposure may still choose SCHG, but they should understand the potential for larger price swings.
