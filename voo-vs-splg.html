VOO vs SPLG

Which S&P 500 ETF may be better for long-term investors?

VOO and SPLG are both ETFs designed to track the S&P 500 Index. Because they follow the same benchmark, their performance is typically very similar. Investors often compare them when deciding which low-cost ETF to use as a core long-term investment.

In simple terms: both VOO and SPLG track the same S&P 500 index. The main differences are provider, structure, and small cost variations.

What do VOO and SPLG have in common?

Both funds track the S&P 500 Index and provide exposure to approximately 500 of the largest U.S. companies. They are widely used by investors who want a simple, diversified portfolio built around the U.S. stock market.

VOO vs SPLG: main differences

Expense ratio

Both ETFs are known for low costs. SPLG has sometimes been slightly cheaper, though the difference is typically very small for long-term investors.

Fund provider

VOO is issued by Vanguard, while SPLG is issued by State Street Global Advisors. Both providers are well-known and manage large ETF families.

Fund size and liquidity

VOO is one of the largest ETFs in the world. SPLG is smaller but still highly liquid and commonly used by investors.

Long-term strategy

Because both ETFs track the same index, many investors simply choose whichever fits their brokerage platform or automatic investment plan.

Is SPLG better than VOO?

SPLG is not necessarily better than VOO. Both provide nearly identical exposure to the S&P 500. The difference for most investors will likely be minimal over long time periods.

Which ETF is better for beginners?

For beginners, either VOO or SPLG can work as a simple long-term investment that tracks the U.S. stock market. The key factor is consistency and long-term investing discipline.
