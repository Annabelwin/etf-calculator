Which ETF Should You Buy?

You don’t need to predict the market. You need a structure that survives it.

Most investors don’t fail because of bad ETFs — they fail because of bad decisions under pressure.

Quick Answer

For most beginners, the decision is simpler than it looks

The default choice

VOO — broad exposure, low cost, easy to hold.

For most long-term investors, this is enough.

If you want total market

VTI — includes everything in the U.S. market.

Slightly broader, similar long-term outcome.

If you want more growth

QQQ — higher upside, higher volatility.

Only if you can tolerate large drawdowns.

If you want income

SCHD — dividend-focused, more stable.

Lower growth, smoother experience.

For most investors, simplicity beats optimization.

A good ETF doesn’t need to do everything. It needs to be strong enough for you to stay invested.
What really matters

This is not just about returns — it’s about risk structure

Every ETF is not just a return profile — it is a behavior test.

  • VOO → robust, survivable, easier to hold
  • QQQ → high upside, but fragile under stress
  • SCHD → smoother ride, but slower growth
  • VTI → diversification as a hedge

The biggest mistake is not picking the wrong ETF — it’s picking one you can’t hold.

Common mistake

Why most beginners choose the wrong ETF

  • They chase performance (QQQ after a strong run)
  • They underestimate volatility
  • They choose something they can’t hold

What looks like opportunity is often just hidden fragility.

ETF paths

Four beginner-friendly strategies

The simple path

Buy VOO and stay invested.

Best for most investors.

The broad market path

Use VTI for full market exposure.

Slight diversification benefit.

The growth path

Add QQQ for higher upside.

Only if you accept volatility.

The income path

Use SCHD for dividends and stability.

Better for income-focused investors.

Decision

How beginners should actually decide

  • Start simple (VOO or VTI)
  • Only add complexity if you understand the trade-off
  • Choose what you can hold during a downturn

Staying invested is the first decision. Everything else is secondary.
