VOO vs SCHD

Which ETF may be better for long-term investors?

VOO and SCHD are both widely used ETFs, but they serve different investing strategies. VOO tracks the S&P 500 and represents broad large-cap U.S. market exposure. SCHD focuses on high-quality dividend-paying companies and is often favored by income-focused investors.

VOO is generally chosen for broad market growth, while SCHD is commonly chosen for dividend income and quality dividend stocks.

What is the difference between VOO and SCHD?

VOO follows the S&P 500 index, meaning it holds many of the largest U.S. companies across different sectors. SCHD tracks a dividend-focused index that selects companies with strong dividend history and financial quality.

VOO vs SCHD: main differences

Growth vs Income

VOO focuses on broad market growth while SCHD focuses on dividend income and stability.

Dividend Yield

SCHD usually offers a higher dividend yield compared to VOO because its strategy targets dividend-paying companies.

Diversification

VOO holds around 500 large companies across many industries, while SCHD holds fewer companies selected for dividend strength.

Strategy Fit

VOO often serves as a core ETF for growth-focused investors, while SCHD is often used for dividend income or income-growth strategies.

Is SCHD better than VOO?

Neither ETF is universally better. VOO may be preferred by investors who want simple broad market exposure. SCHD may be preferred by investors focused on dividend income and dividend growth.

Can you hold both VOO and SCHD?

Yes. Many investors combine VOO and SCHD to balance growth and dividend income in a long-term portfolio.
