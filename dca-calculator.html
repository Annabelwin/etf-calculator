Why DCA Works

Process beats prediction

Dollar-cost averaging spreads your purchases over time, which reduces the pressure to guess the perfect moment to invest. For many investors, the main advantage is better behavior: less hesitation, fewer emotional decisions, and a strategy that is easier to keep following.

Decision Guide

When DCA usually makes sense

  • You want a repeatable monthly investing habit.
  • You are investing for the long term, not trading short-term moves.
  • You prefer discipline over trying to guess market bottoms.
A Common Mistake

Stopping when markets fall

Many investors feel comfortable with DCA in rising markets, then stop investing during downturns. But downturns are often when the process matters most. A rational plan should survive uncomfortable periods, not only easy ones.

Stay the Course

You do not need perfect timing

You need a strategy you can actually follow for years. In many cases, a steady investing habit beats a more “clever” strategy that depends on stress, prediction, or frequent changes.

DCA Calculator FAQ

What is dollar-cost averaging?

Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule, regardless of market price. It helps reduce emotional timing decisions.

Does DCA guarantee better returns?

No. DCA does not guarantee better performance. Its main value is building discipline and reducing the pressure to invest all at once.

Why include ETF fees?

Because even small annual fees reduce your net return, and over long periods that drag compounds against you.
