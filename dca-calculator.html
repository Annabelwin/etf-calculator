Explanation

Why DCA Works

Dollar-cost averaging spreads your purchases over time. That means you do not need to guess the perfect entry point. When prices are lower, the same contribution buys more shares. When prices are higher, you keep your habit going. For many investors, the real benefit is not magic performance, but better behavior and a more durable process.

Decision Guide

How to Decide Whether DCA Fits You

  • Are you investing for the long term rather than trading short-term moves?
  • Do you want a simple plan you can repeat every month?
  • Would consistency help you avoid emotional all-in or all-out decisions?
  • Are you using realistic return assumptions instead of optimistic guesses?
A Common Mistake

Stopping During Downturns

Many investors feel good about DCA in rising markets, then stop when prices fall. But that is often when the discipline matters most. A sound long-term plan should be designed to survive uncomfortable periods, not only easy ones.

Stay the Course

You do not need perfect timing.

You need a strategy you can actually follow for years. In many cases, a steady investing habit beats a more “clever” strategy that depends on prediction, stress, or constant decisions.
