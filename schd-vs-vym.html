SCHD vs VYM

SCHD and VYM are two of the most popular dividend ETFs. Both focus on companies that pay regular dividends, but they follow different strategies and can feel quite different in a long-term portfolio.

What Actually Matters for Long-Term Investors

SCHD and VYM are both built for income investors, but they do not screen stocks the same way.

SCHD focuses more on dividend quality. It selects companies with strong financial health, solid profitability, and a history of sustainable dividend growth. That makes the portfolio more selective and more concentrated.

VYM takes a broader approach. It holds a much larger basket of higher-yield stocks across the U.S. market, which gives it wider diversification but less strict quality filtering.

So the real difference is not just yield. It is strategy. SCHD leans toward dividend growth and quality, while VYM leans toward broader high-yield exposure.

Which One Fits Your Investing Style?

If you prefer a more selective portfolio with stronger fundamentals and better dividend growth discipline, SCHD is often the better fit.

If you want a broader basket of dividend-paying companies and a simpler way to own higher-yield stocks, VYM may feel more suitable.

In practice, both ETFs can work well for long-term income investing. The better choice usually comes down to whether you value quality screening or broader diversification more.

In short: SCHD focuses on dividend quality, while VYM offers broader high-yield diversification.

SCHD vs VYM FAQ

Is SCHD better than VYM?

Neither is universally better. SCHD is often preferred by investors who want stronger dividend quality and growth, while VYM is preferred by those who want broader diversification and higher-yield exposure.

Which ETF usually has the higher dividend yield?

VYM often has a slightly higher dividend yield because it focuses more directly on higher-yield companies across the U.S. market.

Is SCHD more concentrated than VYM?

Yes. SCHD usually holds fewer stocks and applies stricter screening, while VYM owns a broader basket of dividend-paying companies.

Can investors hold both SCHD and VYM?

Yes. Some investors combine both to balance dividend quality, diversification, and current income.
