VOO vs DIA

S&P 500 vs Dow Jones ETF comparison

VOO and DIA both give investors exposure to large U.S. companies, but they track different indexes. VOO follows the S&P 500 and offers broad exposure to around 500 large-cap U.S. companies. DIA tracks the Dow Jones Industrial Average, which includes only 30 large blue-chip companies.

In simple terms: VOO is broader and more diversified, while DIA is more concentrated and tied to the Dow Jones index.

What is the difference between VOO and DIA?

The biggest difference is the number of holdings. VOO tracks a much broader market index, while DIA focuses on only 30 major U.S. companies. This means VOO gives investors more diversification, while DIA reflects the performance of a smaller group of blue-chip stocks.

VOO vs DIA: main differences

Diversification

VOO is much more diversified because it includes around 500 companies. DIA is concentrated in only 30 companies, which makes it more limited in market coverage.

Index structure

VOO follows the S&P 500, which is a broader market benchmark. DIA follows the Dow Jones Industrial Average, which is one of the oldest stock indexes but contains far fewer companies.

Sector exposure

Because VOO includes more companies, its sector exposure is generally broader. DIA may have heavier concentration in certain sectors depending on the Dow’s composition.

Long-term investing fit

Many long-term investors prefer VOO because it gives broader exposure to the U.S. market. DIA may appeal to investors who specifically want exposure to the Dow Jones index.

Is DIA better than VOO?

Not necessarily. DIA may appeal to investors who like the Dow Jones framework and blue-chip focus. However, many long-term investors prefer VOO for its broader diversification and wider market representation.

Which ETF is better for beginners?

Many beginners may find VOO easier to use as a long-term core holding because it offers broader exposure to the U.S. stock market. DIA is usually more niche in comparison.
