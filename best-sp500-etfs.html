What most people miss

Most S&P 500 ETF decisions are not about performance — they are about structure and friction

Investors often spend too much energy comparing tiny differences in ETFs that own the same index. The better question is whether those differences actually change the outcome enough to matter for the kind of investor you are.

Similarity

VOO, IVV, and SPY are much closer than they first appear

All three give exposure to the same S&P 500 companies. For long-term investors, the overlap is enormous.

Why it matters: many people exaggerate the decision because they mistake small differences for structural gaps.
Friction

Small frictions matter more over decades than over weeks

When the holdings are nearly identical, cost and structural efficiency become more meaningful for a long-term path.

Why it matters: the long-term edge often comes from reducing what quietly drags on compounding.
Use case

SPY is not “bad” — it is just not the strongest default

SPY can still make sense for certain trading-oriented uses. It simply answers a different question from long-term default investing.

Behavior

The best S&P 500 ETF is the one that keeps your process simple

When differences are small, clarity matters. Clear default decisions reduce overthinking and help you stay with the plan.
