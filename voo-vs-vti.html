VOO vs VTI

VOO and VTI are two of the most popular Vanguard ETFs, and many long-term investors eventually choose between them. They look similar at first glance, but the key difference is simple: VOO tracks the S&P 500, while VTI owns the entire US stock market.

What Actually Matters for Long-Term Investors

At first glance, VOO and VTI look almost identical. Both are low-cost Vanguard ETFs, both track the US stock market, and both are widely used by long-term investors.

But the real difference is what they actually hold.

VOO tracks the S&P 500, which means it owns around 500 of the largest companies in the United States. These companies dominate the economy — firms like Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and NVIDIA.

VTI goes further. Instead of only large companies, it holds nearly the entire US stock market, including large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap stocks. In total, VTI includes more than 3,000 companies.

So the comparison becomes straightforward: VOO focuses on the largest companies, while VTI aims to represent the full US market.

Which One Fits Your Investing Style?

For many investors, the performance difference between VOO and VTI has historically been small. Large companies make up most of the market’s value, which means the S&P 500 already represents a major portion of the total market.

VOO is often chosen by investors who want a simple and widely recognized benchmark. When people talk about “the market,” they usually mean the S&P 500.

VTI appeals to investors who prefer owning the entire market in one ETF. By including mid-cap and small-cap companies, it provides broader diversification.

In practice, both ETFs work well for long-term investing. The most important factor is not picking the “perfect” ETF, but choosing one that you can keep buying and holding through market cycles.

VOO vs VTI FAQ

Is VTI better than VOO?

Neither ETF is strictly better. VOO focuses on the largest companies in the US, while VTI covers the entire market. For long-term investors, the difference in performance has historically been small.

Do VOO and VTI perform similarly?

Yes, their performance has historically been very close. Large companies dominate the market, which means the S&P 500 already represents most of the total US stock market.

Is VTI more diversified than VOO?

Yes. VTI holds thousands of companies across large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap stocks, while VOO only includes the 500 largest companies.

Which ETF do long-term investors usually choose?

Many investors use either VOO or VTI as a core portfolio holding. Both offer low-cost exposure to the US stock market and are widely used for long-term investing.
