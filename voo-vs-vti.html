VOO vs VTI

VOO and VTI are two of the most popular long-term ETFs, and many investors eventually choose between them. They look similar at first, but the key difference is simple: VOO tracks the S&P 500, while VTI owns nearly the entire U.S. stock market.

Quick Answer

Choose VOO if:

  • You want a simple ETF focused on the 500 largest U.S. companies
  • You like using the S&P 500 as your main long-term benchmark
  • You prefer a very familiar, straightforward core holding

Choose VTI if:

  • You want broader exposure across large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap stocks
  • You prefer owning more of the total U.S. market in one ETF
  • You want slightly more diversification without increasing cost

What Actually Matters for Long-Term Investors

At first glance, VOO and VTI look almost identical. Both are low-cost Vanguard ETFs, both are built for long-term investors, and both give broad exposure to U.S. stocks.

But the real difference is what they actually hold. VOO owns the S&P 500, which means around 500 of the largest companies in the United States. For many investors, that already feels like “the market.”

VTI goes further. It includes not only large companies, but also mid-cap and small-cap stocks. That means thousands of holdings instead of hundreds, and slightly broader diversification across the U.S. economy.

In practice, this is not a dramatic all-or-nothing difference. It is more about whether you want to focus on the largest companies or hold nearly the whole market in one fund.

So the real choice is not “good ETF versus bad ETF.” It is simplicity versus broader coverage.

Which One Fits Your Investing Style?

If you want a simple, widely recognized benchmark that many long-term investors already understand, VOO is often the easier choice. It is clean, familiar, and centered on America’s biggest companies.

If you like the idea of owning more of the market in one ETF, VTI often makes more sense. By adding mid-cap and small-cap exposure, it gives you a fuller version of the U.S. stock market without charging more.

For many investors, the performance gap between VOO and VTI may not feel huge over time. Large companies already make up most of the market’s value, which is why these two ETFs often move in similar ways.

In practice, both ETFs can work very well for long-term investing. The better choice is usually the one you can keep buying and holding through market cycles without second-guessing yourself.

In short: VOO is a simpler large-cap choice, while VTI gives broader total-market diversification at the same low cost.

VOO vs VTI FAQ

VOO vs VTI: which ETF is better?

Neither is universally better. VOO is better for investors who want a simple S&P 500 fund, while VTI is better for investors who want broader exposure to the total U.S. stock market.

Is VTI more diversified than VOO?

Yes. VTI holds thousands of U.S. stocks, including mid-cap and small-cap companies, while VOO holds around 500 large-cap companies in the S&P 500.

Do VOO and VTI perform similarly?

Often, yes. Because large-cap companies dominate the U.S. market, VOO and VTI can move very similarly, though VTI includes a broader slice of the market.

Can beginners choose either VOO or VTI?

Yes. Both are beginner-friendly long-term ETFs. VOO is slightly simpler to understand, while VTI is slightly broader in diversification.
