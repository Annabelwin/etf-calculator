VOO vs VTI

Which ETF may be better for long-term investors?

VOO and VTI are two extremely popular ETFs from Vanguard that many long-term investors consider when building a portfolio. While both funds track the U.S. stock market, they follow different indexes and offer slightly different exposure.

In simple terms: VOO tracks the S&P 500, while VTI tracks the entire U.S. stock market.

What is the difference between VOO and VTI?

VOO tracks the S&P 500 Index, which includes about 500 of the largest publicly traded companies in the United States. This makes it a popular choice for investors who want exposure to large, established businesses.

VTI tracks the CRSP US Total Market Index and includes thousands of companies across large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap stocks. This provides broader exposure to the entire U.S. equity market.

VOO vs VTI: key differences

1. Market coverage

VOO focuses on large-cap companies in the S&P 500, while VTI includes nearly the entire U.S. stock market, including smaller companies.

2. Diversification

VTI is slightly more diversified because it includes thousands of companies, whereas VOO includes about 500 large-cap companies.

3. Long-term performance

Historically, the performance of VOO and VTI has been very similar because large companies make up a large portion of the total U.S. market.

4. Portfolio simplicity

Many investors prefer VOO for its simplicity and focus on the largest U.S. companies. Others choose VTI because it provides exposure to the entire market with a single ETF.

Is VTI better than VOO?

Neither ETF is strictly better. VTI offers slightly broader diversification, while VOO focuses on the largest and most established companies in the U.S. economy. Both are commonly used as long-term core holdings.

Can you hold both VOO and VTI?

Some investors hold both, but because VOO is already a large portion of VTI, holding both does not significantly increase diversification. Many investors simply choose one as their core ETF.
