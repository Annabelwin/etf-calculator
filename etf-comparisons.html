Decision Support

What investors are really deciding

On the surface, ETF comparison pages look like ticker-versus-ticker pages. The real decision is usually deeper than that.

  • Not just fund vs fund — but simplicity vs complexity.
  • Not just recent performance — but whether you can stay invested through disappointment.
  • Not just growth potential — but concentration risk and behavioral fragility.
  • Not just current income — but future total return and optionality.
The biggest mistake is often not choosing the “wrong” ETF. It is choosing a structure that looks exciting today but becomes hard to hold when the market gets uncomfortable.
The Four Masters

The thinking behind this comparison center

These pages are not organized as a random list of tickers. They are organized around durable investing ideas that help people make better decisions.

Bogle

Costs matter. Simplicity matters. A comparison only helps if it makes it easier to stay in a low-cost, sensible structure for decades.

Buffett

A good decision should be understandable. If the structure is hard to explain, it is often hard to hold when markets get rough.

Munger

Inversion matters. Ask not only “What has upside?” but also “What is most likely to make me do something foolish later?”

Taleb

The goal is not to predict. The goal is to avoid fragility. Comparisons should help you reduce hidden downside while keeping long-term upside open.

Barbell Structure

The comparison center follows a barbell approach: one side helps you make a fast, calm default decision; the other side lets you go deeper into risk, behavior, and structural tradeoffs when needed.

Left side: quick default answers

Start with pages like VOO vs SPY or VOO vs VTI if you want a simple decision path without noise.

Right side: deeper tradeoff analysis

Go into pages like VOO vs QQQ or VOO vs SCHD if you need to think about concentration, behavior, income, and second-order effects.
