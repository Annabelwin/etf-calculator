Quick Answer

Choose IVV if:

  • You want lower fees for long-term investing
  • You are building a buy-and-hold portfolio
  • You prefer a simple, cost-efficient core ETF

Choose SPY if:

  • You care about liquidity and tight bid-ask spreads
  • You trade more actively
  • You want one of the most heavily traded ETFs in the world

What Actually Matters for Long-Term Investors

When people compare IVV vs SPY, the key point is that both ETFs track the exact same index: the S&P 500.

IVV has a lower expense ratio, which can make a meaningful difference over long periods, especially for investors who consistently invest and hold.

SPY, on the other hand, is one of the most actively traded ETFs in the world. Its high liquidity makes it especially useful for traders and institutions.

So while performance is nearly identical before fees, the real difference comes down to cost efficiency versus trading flexibility.

Which One Fits Your Investing Style?

If your goal is long-term investing with minimal costs, IVV is often the more efficient choice.

If you are more active in the market and care about execution, liquidity, and trading volume, SPY may be more suitable.

In practice, long-term investors often lean toward IVV, while traders and institutions tend to prefer SPY.

In short, IVV vs SPY is less about which ETF is better and more about how you plan to use it.

In short: IVV offers lower costs for long-term investors, while SPY provides unmatched liquidity for active trading.

IVV vs SPY FAQ

IVV vs SPY: which ETF is better?

Neither is universally better. IVV is typically better for long-term investors due to lower fees, while SPY is preferred by traders because of its high liquidity.

Do IVV and SPY have the same holdings?

Yes. Both track the S&P 500, so their holdings are nearly identical.

Why is SPY more popular than IVV?

SPY was the first ETF listed in the U.S. and remains one of the most heavily traded, making it extremely liquid.

Is IVV cheaper than SPY?

Yes. IVV has a lower expense ratio, which can reduce long-term costs for investors.
