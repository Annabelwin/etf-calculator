VOO vs IVV vs SPY

VOO, IVV and SPY are three of the most popular ETFs in the world. All three track the S&P 500 index, which means they invest in the same group of large U.S. companies.

Why Are There Three S&P 500 ETFs?

The S&P 500 index is one of the most widely followed stock market indexes in the world. Different asset managers created ETFs to track this index. SPY was the first S&P 500 ETF launched in 1993 and became extremely popular among traders and institutions. Later, Vanguard launched VOO and BlackRock launched IVV with lower expense ratios aimed at long-term investors.

The Main Difference Investors Care About

Because all three ETFs track the same index, their performance over long periods is almost identical. The main difference is the expense ratio. VOO and IVV charge about 0.03% annually, while SPY charges about 0.09%. That difference may seem small, but over decades lower fees can slightly improve long-term returns.

Which One Do Long-Term Investors Choose?

Many long-term investors prefer VOO or IVV because of the lower expense ratio. SPY is still widely used, especially by traders and institutions, because it has extremely high trading volume and liquidity. For someone building a long-term portfolio and investing regularly, VOO or IVV are often the most common choices.
