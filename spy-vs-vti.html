SPY vs VTI

SPY and VTI are both widely used U.S. stock market ETFs, but they are built for slightly different purposes. One tracks the S&P 500, while the other covers nearly the entire U.S. stock market.

What Actually Matters for Long-Term Investors

At first glance, SPY and VTI may look similar because both give investors exposure to the U.S. stock market. But the real difference is what part of the market you are buying and how much you pay to own it.

SPY tracks the S&P 500, which means it holds about 500 of the largest publicly traded companies in the United States. In practice, that gives you exposure to the biggest and most influential firms in the U.S. economy.

VTI goes further. It holds thousands of U.S. companies, not just the large ones. That means you get large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap exposure in a single ETF.

For many long-term investors, the biggest practical difference is that VTI offers broader diversification and comes with a lower expense ratio. SPY, by contrast, remains more popular among traders because of its deep liquidity and massive trading volume.

Which One Fits Your Investing Style?

If you want a simple buy-and-hold ETF that captures almost the entire U.S. market, VTI will often make more sense. It is low-cost, broad, and easy to hold for decades.

If you specifically want the S&P 500 and care about trading liquidity, SPY may still be attractive. It remains one of the most heavily traded ETFs in the world.

For long-term investors, though, the decision often comes down to this: do you want just the largest companies, or the whole market? If you prefer broader exposure and lower fees, VTI usually has the edge.

SPY vs VTI FAQ

Is VTI more diversified than SPY?

Yes. VTI holds thousands of U.S. companies, while SPY only holds the 500 companies in the S&P 500.

Why do investors compare SPY and VTI?

They compare them because both provide broad U.S. stock exposure, but one focuses on the S&P 500 while the other covers the total market.

Is VTI cheaper than SPY?

Yes. VTI generally has a lower expense ratio, which matters more for long-term investors than for short-term traders.

Which ETF is better for long-term investing?

Many long-term investors prefer VTI because of its broader diversification and lower ongoing cost.
