Why Investors Compare VOO and IVV

At first glance, VOO and IVV appear identical. Both track the S&P 500, which means they hold roughly the same 500 large companies in the United States. If Apple, Microsoft or Amazon moves, both ETFs move almost the same way.

Because of that, long-term performance between the two funds is extremely similar. Over many years the return difference is usually just a tiny fraction of a percent.

So why do investors still compare them? Mostly because they want to confirm that there is no hidden difference before committing to a long-term position.

What Actually Matters for Long-Term Investors

For long-term investors, the practical differences between VOO and IVV are minimal. Both have extremely low expense ratios, strong liquidity, and are managed by some of the largest asset managers in the world.

VOO is issued by Vanguard, a firm famous for promoting low-cost index investing. Many investors feel comfortable with Vanguard's philosophy and long history of investor-first policies.

IVV is issued by BlackRock under the iShares brand. BlackRock is the largest asset manager in the world and IVV is also one of the biggest ETFs globally.

In reality, the choice often comes down to personal preference, platform availability, or which ETF is easier to buy through a particular broker.
